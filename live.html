Lowcountry Neighborhoods and Real Estate

A plain-spoken look at where people live around Charleston, how each area feels day to day, and the local firms that know the market.

Downtown Charleston

Living on the peninsula means walking to dinner, biking to work, and sharing the sidewalks with visitors. Homes run from single houses with side piazzas to carriage-house apartments, and lots are tight. The City of Charleston handles permitting and the historic district rules that come with owning in the old city.

Mount Pleasant

Across the Ravenel Bridge, Mount Pleasant offers newer construction, strong schools, and quick access to Shem Creek, Sullivan’s Island, and Isle of Palms. The Old Village keeps a small-town feel near the harbor, while neighborhoods along Highway 17 trend larger and more suburban. The Town of Mount Pleasant site covers zoning, parks, and town services.

James Island and West Ashley

These two sit in the middle of everything. James Island is laid-back with marsh views and a short drive to Folly Beach, and West Ashley has established neighborhoods, the Greenway for walking and biking, and some of the better values close to downtown.

Daniel Island, Johns Island, and Summerville

Daniel Island is a planned community with parks, trails, and a compact town center. Johns Island still has rural stretches under live oaks, though growth is steady. Summerville, inland to the northwest, trades a longer commute for more space and a historic downtown of its own. Families comparing areas can check school zones through the Charleston County School District.

Working With Local Firms

The Lowcountry market is block-by-block, and flood zones, elevation, and bridge access matter as much as square footage. Long-standing brokerages like Carolina One Real Estate, The Cassina Group, Dunes Properties, Daniel Ravenel Sotheby’s International Realty, and William Means Real Estate know those details firsthand.
